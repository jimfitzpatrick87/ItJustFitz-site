Straight answers.

The questions people actually ask before hiring me.

Do I have to sign up for a monthly plan?

No. Most clients do, because it's the difference between a site that works and a site nobody's watching — but you have a real alternative. Build & Handoff is the project price plus $400: I deploy the site into your own hosting account, transfer the code, hand over every login, write you a runbook, and walk you through all of it on a call. Then it's yours. It costs a bit more than the care plan route because handing a site over is more work at delivery than keeping it. What you give up is monitoring, backups, uptime alerts, emergency fixes, and included update hours — and AI features aren't available on it, for the reason in the next question. You can join a care plan later for a $400 audit fee.

Why can't I get the AI features without a plan?

Because they'd break, and you'd be the one holding the bill. An AI assistant on your site needs live API keys and someone watching what it costs to run. Call tracking is a running subscription. Google Business Profile monitoring is, by definition, ongoing. I'm not willing to hand you something that quietly runs up a bill or quietly stops working the day I'm no longer looking at it. If you want those, the care plan is what makes them responsible to sell you.

Who owns my domain?

You do, always. It's registered at Porkbun in your name, on your card, in your account. I'm added as a delegate so I can manage the DNS, and that's it. If you fire me tomorrow, nothing needs to be transferred — you already own it. I'd rather earn the next year than hold your address hostage.

What happens if I stop the care plan?

You get written notice and a 30-day grace period, and your site stays online the whole time. After that, hosting and monitoring end. Your domain is still yours and your site files are still yours — ask and I'll send them, no charge. I'm not going to take a local business offline without warning.

Can I update the site myself?

On a care plan, you send me the change and I make it — text edits, photo swaps, new hours, updated contact info. Essential includes two hours a year, Standard adds a block each quarter. Most small businesses use less than that. If you want to be in there editing weekly, I'm honestly the wrong fit and you'd be happier on Squarespace or Wix — I'll tell you that in the first call rather than sell you something that annoys you.

Won't the AI features cost me a fortune to run?

No, and I've built the whole practice around making sure of it. I use the cheapest model that does the job, run what I can on a local model at no per-use cost, cap the length of every response, and log the cost of every single call so it's visible instead of a surprise. For a typical small business site, the AI runs for less per month than one streaming subscription. You're paying me to build it, not a tech giant to run it.

How long does a build take?

Two to four weeks for Essential and Standard, assuming your copy and photos arrive on time. That last part is the whole ballgame — the single most common reason a two-week project turns into a four-month one is waiting on content. If you don't have copy and photos ready, I can handle that for $500 and we'll stay on schedule.

What if I need a new page later?

I'll quote it flat before I start — usually a few hundred dollars depending on what's on it. New pages, design changes, and new features sit outside the care plan's update hours on purpose, because a new page is real design work, not a text edit. Anything outside your plan is $150/hour, always estimated and approved first. You will never get a surprise invoice from me.

Why not just use Squarespace or Wix?

Sometimes you should, and I'll say so. If you want to edit your own site every week or you're selling products online, go use one of those — you'll be happier and it'll be cheaper. What you get here instead is a site that loads fast, gets set up properly in local search, is wired to actually capture leads and tell you where they came from, and has someone answering the phone when it breaks. That's a different product, not a fancier version of the same one.

Do you record my phone calls?

Not unless you specifically ask for it and take that decision to your own attorney first. Call tracking platforms often turn recording on by default; I turn it off. Pennsylvania law requires every party on a call to consent to being recorded, and it's a criminal statute, not a slap on the wrist. I'm not switching that on for you as a convenience.

What happens if you get hit by a bus?

Fair question, and it's why your domain is in your name and your site is plain HTML in a code repository rather than locked inside a platform only I can log into. I keep a written recovery document listing every account and how to get into it. If I'm gone, you own your domain, your files, and enough documentation for any competent developer to pick it up. Ask me for a copy any time.

I already have a website. Can you just fix it?

Sometimes. Send me the link. If it's built on something reasonable, a rebuild is often cheaper and faster than untangling it — and if your domain is stuck at a registrar that overcharges you, moving it is usually the first thing worth doing, and it takes about an hour. I'll tell you honestly if you're better off keeping what you have.
